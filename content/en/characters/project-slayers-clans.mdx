## What Are Clans in Project Slayers?

Joining the right group can change how fast you level, how often you clear bosses, and how much fun you have in Project Slayers. Project Slayers clans — the player-run groups the game presents through its Family system — give you a shared tag, a steady crew to farm with, and small progression perks that quietly add up over hundreds of hours. If you are stuck grinding solo or tired of rolling the dice on public lobbies, learning how Project Slayers clans work is the fastest way to fix both problems.

Project Slayers is a Roblox action RPG inspired by Demon Slayer. Most of your raw power comes from your race (Human, Demon, or Hybrid, depending on the current update), your Breathing Style or Demon Art, your weapon and gear, and your level. Clans sit on top of that foundation as a social layer, not a replacement for it.

| System | What it decides | Can you change it? | Solo or shared? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Race | Core abilities, skill tree direction, transformation tools | Only with rare in-game items or resets | Solo |
| Breathing Style / Demon Art | Your combat moveset and playstyle | Rerollable with in-game items | Solo |
| Weapon and gear | Damage, defense, and stat scaling | Swappable any time | Solo |
| Clan (Family) | Social group, name tag, shared perks, group content | Join and leave rules depend on the clan | Shared |

Because Project Slayers clans are player-run, quality varies wildly. A mid-level clan with twenty active members will do far more for you than a high-level clan whose leader quit months ago. That is why "join the biggest name you can find" is bad advice — activity beats prestige almost every time.

## How to Join or Create a Clan in Project Slayers

For new and mid-game players, joining is almost always the smarter move. Established clans already have the perks unlocked, the officers who organize runs, and the veterans who can explain why your build feels weak. Most recruiting happens in the in-game chat, in community Discord servers, and through community-run wikis and forums.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open the Family/Clan menu from the main interface | It shows your options: create, search, or review invitations |
| 2 | Check the clan's level and listed perks before accepting | Clan level usually determines the size of the passive bonuses |
| 3 | Ask in chat or a community Discord for open clans | The most active groups recruit socially, not from a static list |
| 4 | Tell officers your race, level, and main build | They can match you to content they actually need help with |
| 5 | Accept the invite and equip the tag | Your tag shows on your character and in chat |

### Creating Your Own Clan

If you would rather build something from scratch, the game lets you found a family of your own. Founding costs in-game currency and requires you to meet a level gate, and those exact numbers have shifted across updates — always confirm the current values in the Family menu before you spend anything.

| Requirement | Typical detail | Practical tip |
|---|---|---|
| In-game currency | One-time creation fee paid from your own wallet | Keep a buffer for upgrades after founding |
| Character level | Minimum level needed to found a family | Level up first, then found — don't rush |
| Unique name and tag | Must follow Roblox naming and chat rules | Short tags are easier to recognize in lobbies |
| Recruiting | You handle invites, promotions, and removals | Start with four or five active friends, not strangers |

A brand-new clan with no perks is a tough sell, so recruit people you already play with. Five consistent players who log in daily will out-progress twenty random invites who never return.

## Clan Ranks, Roles, and Permissions

Project Slayers clans run on a rank ladder. Exact titles vary slightly between groups, but the structure is consistent: one person owns the family, a small officer core keeps it running, and everyone else fills out the roster.

| Rank | Typical permissions | Usually held by | Best practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leader / Owner | Rename, promote, kick, manage settings | The founder | Stay active or hand leadership off early |
| Elder / Officer | Invite, promote, organize events | Trusted long-term members | Promote the people who actually recruit |
| Member | Full access to clan perks and the tag | Standard rank | Contribute to group content regularly |
| Recruit / Trial | Limited access, sometimes no tag yet | New joins | Prove activity in your first week |

If you are aiming for officer, the fastest route is simple: be online during peak hours, help newer members through early bosses, and never ask for free gear. Community reports consistently say that helpfulness gets promoted faster than raw level.

## Clan Perks and Progression Benefits

This is where clans earn their keep. Perks are generally modest on their own, but they compound across a long play session — and the social benefits are often bigger than the mechanical ones.

| Benefit | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Passive stat / XP bonuses | Small boosts that scale with clan level | Every member, every session |
| Visible name tag | Displays your clan beside your character name | Players building a reputation |
| Group content access | Boss farming, raid nights, dungeon runs | Mid-game and endgame players |
| Trading and gear help | Find members with spare weapons and materials | Newer players short on resources |
| Build advice | Veterans explain Breathing Style and Demon Art combos | Anyone who feels stuck |

Do not expect a clan to carry you. Treat perks as a multiplier on your own effort: a clan bonus on top of steady questing is meaningful, while the same bonus on top of standing in town all day is not.

| Clan level range | What to expect | Realistic goal for members |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Few or no perks, mostly a social tag | Find reliable teammates and learn the game |
| Mid | Modest passive bonuses, organized farming | Finish story content and unlock stronger styles |
| High | Stronger bonuses, experienced officer core | Push endgame bosses and PvP improvement |

## How to Pick the Right Clan (and Avoid Bad Ones)

The right clan depends on how you play, not on which one has the loudest recruitment message.

| Player type | What to look for | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| New / casual | Patient veterans and low activity requirements | Clans that demand daily logins |
| Mid-game grinder | Active farming schedule and shared materials | Groups with dead chats |
| PvP-focused | Members who duel and scrim regularly | Purely social clans |
| Demon main | Members running Demon Art content | Clans built entirely around Human builds |
| Endgame completionist | Officers who organize boss rotations | Clans with no veteran core |

Watch for these red flags before you commit:

- The leader has not logged in for weeks.
- There is no Discord, group chat, or any way to coordinate outside the game.
- Officers ask for your rarest items as "dues" or entry fees.
- Every member is your level with no one to learn from.
- The tag is mandatory, but nobody ever organizes anything.

Player experience is worth trusting here. Ask a recruit how long they have been in the clan and whether runs actually happen — a two-minute conversation saves weeks of dead weight.

## FAQ

**What are Project Slayers clans?**
They are the player-run family groups inside the game, built around the Family system. Each clan has its own name, tag, rank ladder, and a set of perks that unlocks as the group levels up.

**Do Project Slayers clans give stat boosts?**
Yes, though they are intentionally small. Most clans offer minor passive bonuses tied to clan level, plus practical advantages like easy access to farming groups and spare gear.

**Can I leave a clan and join another?**
Leaving is generally done through the Family menu, and officers can also remove members. If you plan to switch, tell someone first — the community is small, and reputation travels faster than you would expect.

**Do I need to be a certain level to join?**
Individual clans set their own standards, and many expect a minimum level or an active schedule. If you are brand new, look for beginner-friendly clans that advertise open recruitment, then upgrade once your build comes together.

Wherever you land, treat clans in Project Slayers as a long-term investment. Show up, help with runs, and the perks — and the people — will do the rest.
