Two demons at the same level can play completely differently in Project Slayers, and the difference usually comes down to one system: Project Slayers Demon Arts. Also known as Blood Demon Arts, these are the demon-exclusive abilities that decide your damage, your mobility, and how you control a fight.

If you are grinding levels, farming bosses, or dueling in PvP, understanding Project Slayers Demon Arts is the biggest single upgrade you can make to your build. This guide covers how the system works, how to roll and reroll your art, which archetypes fit which playstyle, and how to spend skill points without wasting them.

One quick note before we start: Project Slayers updates regularly, and patches can change the art pool, rarity tiers, costs, and damage numbers. Treat any exact figure you see online — including in this article's general advice — as version-dependent, and verify the current state in-game before spending resources.

## What Are Blood Demon Arts in Project Slayers?

Blood Demon Arts are the demon-side ability system. Where a Slayer picks a Breathing Style, a demon rolls for a Demon Art, and that art becomes the backbone of their entire kit. It defines your range, your damage type, your crowd control, and often your mobility.

Each art is built around a theme and gives you a small set of active moves plus passive traits that shape how you fight. Some kits lean into close-range pressure, others into projectiles and zoning, and others into utility like healing, escapes, or area denial.

A few core facts apply across the board:

- **Demon-exclusive.** Slayers cannot use Blood Demon Arts, and demons cannot use Breathing Styles. Your path choice locks this in.
- **Rolled, not chosen.** You do not pick the art you want from a menu. You roll for it, which means luck plays a real role in the early game.
- **Rarity matters, but skill matters more.** Rarer arts generally have stronger or more flexible kits, but a well-played common art beats a poorly played rare one most of the time.
- **Everything is upgradeable.** Arts have moves that scale with investment, so an art you dislike on day one can feel completely different once leveled.

The Project Slayers Wiki lists arts, their moves, and rarity details, but like most community wikis it can lag behind patches, so cross-check anything that looks outdated.

## How to Obtain and Reroll Project Slayers Demon Arts

Most players get their first art by rolling a Demon Art spin. Spins are generally earned through progression, events, or purchased with Robux, though the exact sources and costs change between updates. Here is the practical sequence most experienced demons follow.

| Step | What to Do | What to Watch For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Confirm your path | Make sure you are committed to the demon path before investing | You cannot swap freely once you have built around an art |
| 2. Stock spins | Earn or buy spins before you start rolling | Rolling one at a time with no backup is a common beginner mistake |
| 3. Roll | Use a spin and see what art you land on | Rarity tiers shift with updates; a "bad" roll may be rebalanced later |
| 4. Test the kit | Head to a safe area or low-threat mobs and use every move once | Learn cooldowns and range before judging the art |
| 5. Reroll if needed | Spend another spin to replace the art | Rerolling costs resources you could spend on levels and gear |
| 6. Commit and level | Put skill points into the moves you actually use | Spread thin and you will be weak at everything |

A common community recommendation is to bank several spins before rolling. Player experience suggests that rolling in bulk makes it easier to accept a mediocre result and keep going, rather than panic-rerolling a decent art you never gave a fair chance.

Also worth knowing: rerolling Project Slayers Demon Arts is not a one-time decision. You can keep rerolling as you earn more spins, so an early low-tier art is not a permanent sentence.

## Demon Arts vs Breathing Styles: How the Two Paths Compare

The two systems look symmetrical, but they reward different habits. Use this table as a quick reference when deciding how to spend your time.

| Factor | Blood Demon Arts (Demon) | Breathing Styles (Slayer) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you get it | Rolled with spins | Unlocked and upgraded through the Slayer path |
| Rarity system | Yes, arts vary in rarity | Yes, styles vary in strength and cost |
| Skill point investment | Required for each move | Required for each move |
| Typical playstyle | High burst, disruption, unusual utility | Clean combos, mobility, sword-focused pressure |
| PvP reputation | Strong burst and crowd control | Strong combo consistency and movement |
| PvE farming | Excellent with area and multi-target kits | Excellent with sustained damage |
| Main weakness | Reliant on hitting cooldowns and setup | Reliant on staying in melee range |

Neither path is strictly better. Community reports generally agree that the "best" side is the one that matches how you like to fight — aggressive demons who enjoy burst windows do better on the demon path, while players who like precise sword combos tend toward Slayers.

## Demon Art Archetypes: Find the Kit That Fits You

You do not need to memorize every art to play well. Instead, learn the five archetypes most Project Slayers Demon Arts fall into, then judge each new roll against the one that suits you.

| Archetype | How It Feels | Best For | Watch Out For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Close-range bruiser | High burst, short range, heavy pressure | Duelists who like trading hits | Getting zoned out by ranged kits |
| Projectile pressure | Ranged pokes and chip damage | Players with good aim and patience | Low burst if you miss your windows |
| Crowd control / area denial | Traps, wide hits, lockdown | Boss farming and group fights | Long cooldowns that punish bad timing |
| Mobility skirmisher | Dashes, repositioning, hit-and-run | PvP players who like to dictate range | Lower raw damage per hit |
| Utility / sustain | Healing, buffs, defensive tools | Support-focused and survival builds | Slower clear speed solo |

A good exercise after every roll: take the art to a low-level area and count how long it takes to clear a group of mobs. Then take it into a duel against a friend. If it feels awkward in both, reroll. If it feels great in one and bad in the other, you have found your specialty.

## Skill Points, Mastery, and Where to Invest

Skill points are limited, and dumping them evenly across every move is the fastest way to a weak build. Most players get better results by picking one or two core moves and treating the rest as situational.

| Stage | Priority | Why It Pays Off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early levels | Your cheapest, most reliable damage move | Gives a dependable combo starter while you learn the kit |
| Early levels | One defensive or escape option | Surviving fights teaches you more than dying fast |
| Mid levels | Your main combo extender | Turns single hits into real damage chains |
| Mid levels | A crowd-control or area move | Speeds up grinding dramatically |
| Late levels | Your ultimate or highest-impact move | Wins duels and finishes bosses |
| Any time | Avoid spreading points | Half-leveled moves underperform at every stage |

Player experience suggests that mastery comes from repetition rather than raw stats. Run the same opening combo dozens of times against easy mobs until the inputs are automatic, then add the second and third moves. Muscle memory beats a slightly rarer art every time.

## Practical Tips for PvP and PvE With Project Slayers Demon Arts

The same art can feel broken in one mode and mediocre in another. Adjust your approach instead of blaming the roll.

| Scenario | Smart Approach | Mistake to Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grinding mobs | Chain an area move into your fastest damage move | Burning your escape move on cooldown for no reason |
| Farming bosses | Save burst for the phase where the boss is vulnerable | Blowing cooldowns at the start of the fight |
| 1v1 duels | Bait out the opponent's dodge, then commit to your combo | Opening with your slowest, most telegraphed move |
| Group PvP | Stay at the edge of the fight and pick targets | Diving into the middle and getting focused |
| Getting ganked | Reposition first, then counterattack | Trading hits when you are outnumbered |

Additional habits that consistently help:

- **Learn your cooldowns cold.** Know which move you can always rely on and which one is a gamble.
- **Pair your art with the right gear and clan bonuses.** Your art is only part of the build; equipment and passive bonuses fill the gaps.
- **Practice in a private or low-population server** before testing a new combo in ranked-style fights.
- **Watch the patch notes.** A buff or nerf can move an art up or down several tiers overnight.
- **Keep one backup spin.** If a patch guts your favorite kit, you want an immediate reroll available.

Project Slayers runs on Roblox, so you will need the official Roblox client and an account to play — you can grab both from [Roblox's official platform](https://www.roblox.com). Keeping your client updated also avoids the mismatched-version errors that occasionally interrupt play sessions.

## FAQ

**How do you unlock Project Slayers Demon Arts?**

You unlock them by committing to the demon path and rolling with a Demon Art spin. Spins are generally earned through progression or bought with Robux, and the exact sources change with updates. Once you roll, that art becomes your active kit and can be upgraded with skill points.

**Can you change your Demon Art after rolling it?**

Yes. Rerolling replaces your current art with a new random one. Because rerolls consume spins or resources, most players bank a few before deciding, and many keep one spare in case a patch weakens the art they were using.

**Which Project Slayers Demon Arts are the best?**

There is no permanent answer. Rarity and balance shift with every major update, and player experience shows that a well-practiced mid-tier art usually outperforms a rarer art the player has not learned. Pick an archetype you enjoy — burst, ranged, crowd control, mobility, or utility — then master it.

**Are Demon Arts better than Breathing Styles?**

They are different, not better. Blood Demon Arts tend to offer burst damage, disruption, and unusual utility, while Breathing Styles reward consistent melee combos and movement. The stronger choice is the one that matches your playstyle and the content you spend the most time in.

## Final Thoughts

Your Demon Art is the identity of your demon build in Project Slayers. Roll deliberately, test every kit before you judge it, invest skill points into a small number of moves, and keep a spare spin for the inevitable balance patch. Do that, and you will get far more out of Project Slayers Demon Arts than players who chase the rarest roll they see mentioned in a tier list.
