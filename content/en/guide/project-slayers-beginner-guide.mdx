## What Kind of Game Are You Actually Playing?

Project Slayers is a Demon Slayer-inspired action RPG on Roblox. You pick a path, learn a breathing style or a demon art, and grind through quests, NPC kills, and story bosses to unlock stronger abilities. Combat is combo-based: light attacks chain together, skills have cooldowns, and blocking plus dodging decides most exchanges.

Because the game is updated regularly, exact item names, prices, and unlock requirements shift over time. Community reports suggest the safest mindset is to treat every guide — including this one — as a set of priorities rather than a checklist of fixed numbers. The [Project Slayers Wiki](https://project-slayers.fandom.com/) is the community's running record of those changes, and the game itself lives on the [official Roblox platform](https://www.roblox.com/).

## Your First Hour: Race, Bloodline, and Setup Decisions

The first thing most new players do is spend cash on cosmetics and rerolls they do not understand yet. Do not. Instead, claim your starter gear, finish the tutorial quests, and learn the movement and combat controls before you make any permanent choice.

| Decision | What It Affects | Beginner-Friendly Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Race | Core stats and available abilities | Pick a Human-style race for a straightforward start; demon and hybrid variants raise the difficulty curve |
| Bloodline | Passive perks such as survivability or damage | Keep the first roll you get unless it is clearly useless — rerolls get expensive |
| Appearance & name | Cosmetics only | Spend nothing here early |
| Skill points | Which abilities you can actually use | Concentrate points into one playstyle instead of spreading them thin |
| First weapon | Damage and combo feel | Use quest rewards until you unlock a better blade |

Two rules carry most beginners through their first few hours:

- **Do not reroll blindly.** Every reroll you burn early is currency you cannot spend on a breathing trainer later.
- **Do not fight far above your level.** NPC enemies that take ten seconds to kill are wasting your time, not training it.

Bloodlines matter more in the long run than most new players assume, but a mediocre bloodline played well beats a great one played badly. Player experience consistently points to survivability perks being the friendliest starting option, because staying alive is how you learn boss patterns.

## Leveling Fast: Quests, Routes, and Where to Spend

Leveling in Project Slayers comes from three sources: story quests, repeatable NPC grinding, and event or boss content. Story quests should almost always come first, because they hand out unlocks that grinding can never give you.

| Stage | Primary Goal | Best Use of Time | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| First session | Learn controls, finish tutorial | Story quests near your spawn area | Buying cosmetics or random items |
| Early game | Hit a stable leveling rhythm | Quest chains plus NPCs you can kill quickly | Wandering into high-level zones |
| Mid game | Unlock and master your breathing style | Trainer quests, style mastery, weapon upgrades | Hoarding currency you never spend |
| Late game | Bosses, PvP, trading | Group content and gear hunting | Solo pushing content built for teams |

A practical grinding loop looks like this:

1. Take every available story quest in your current area.
2. Kill NPCs that die in a few combos — speed beats difficulty for experience.
3. Return to the quest giver immediately; do not stack unfinished quests.
4. Spend earnings on your breathing trainer or a weapon upgrade, not on cosmetics.
5. Move to the next area only when kills start feeling slow.

Where should your early currency go? Priorities vary by version, but community consensus is consistent: breathing style unlocks and mastery come first, weapon upgrades second, consumables third. Cosmetic rerolls sit dead last.

## Breathing Styles Compared: Picking Your First One

Your breathing style is the single biggest decision in the early game. Most styles are obtained through a trainer NPC, a quest chain, or in-game currency, and rarer styles usually demand more effort. The table below reflects general community impressions of how each archetype plays rather than hard numbers.

| Style | Playstyle | Difficulty | Good For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | Balanced combos, forgiving range | Low | First-time players |
| Flame | Heavy hits, simple chains | Low–Medium | Players who like burst damage |
| Thunder | Fast, aggressive, mobile | Medium | Duelists and PvP learners |
| Beast | Chaotic, movement-heavy | Medium | Players who enjoy unpredictability |
| Mist | Evasive, tricky timing | Medium–High | Reactive, dodge-focused players |
| Insect | Technical, ranged pressure | High | Patient players who like setups |
| Wind | Wide-hitting area damage | Medium | Farming groups of NPCs |
| Serpent | Unusual angles, hard to read | High | Confident combo players |
| Sound | Utility and support tools | Medium | Team-oriented players |

If you are undecided, pick Water or Flame. They teach the fundamentals — spacing, combo timing, and cooldown management — without punishing small mistakes. You can always chase a flashier style once you understand how the game's combat actually flows.

Weapon choice matters almost as much. Rarity usually tracks damage and animation quality, so a rare blade you can actually use is worth more than a legendary one locked behind content you cannot clear. Do not trade away your only decent weapon for a cosmetic upgrade.

## Slayer or Demon? Understanding the Two Paths

Most players start on the slayer side and meet demons as enemies. Later, demon options open up new abilities and a different combat rhythm entirely. Here is the honest comparison.

| Factor | Slayer Path | Demon Path |
|---|---|---|
| Core toolkit | Breathing styles | Blood demon arts |
| Combat feel | Structured combos, defined roles | Unusual abilities, high burst |
| Learning curve | Gentler | Steeper |
| Team value | Strong support and DPS options | Strong pressure and disruption |
| Best for | New players | Experienced players starting a second character |

If this is your first character, stay a slayer. Learn the map, learn the quest flow, and learn how blocking and dodging really work. Then, if you want a fresh challenge, roll a demon and you will already know half the game.

## Five Mistakes That Slow New Players Down

Community reports and player experience point to the same handful of errors again and again.

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Spreading skill points everywhere | No ability hits hard enough | Commit to one build |
| Skipping story quests | Misses permanent unlocks | Clear quest chains first |
| Rerolling constantly | Drains currency and patience | Keep a decent roll and move on |
| Grinding NPCs far above your level | Slow kills, slow progress | Fight enemies you can combo down |
| Ignoring blocking and dodging | Death in every serious fight | Practice defense before offense |

Add two social habits to that list. First, join an active clan or family early — group grinding and shared knowledge beat solo guessing. Second, learn how trading works before you accept any offer, because the item economy rewards players who know what things are worth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should a Project Slayers beginner guide tell me to do first?**
Finish the tutorial and early story quests before spending anything. Those quests unlock systems you will use for the rest of your run, and they cost nothing but time.

**Which breathing style is best for a brand-new player?**
Water and Flame are the most forgiving starting styles. Both teach spacing and combo timing without demanding perfect execution, and neither locks you out of later options.

**Should I reroll my race or bloodline immediately?**
Usually no. Rerolls are a currency sink, and a solid build matters more than a perfect roll. Play a few hours first so you know what you actually want.

**How do I stop dying so much in Project Slayers?**
Practice blocking, dodging, and disengaging instead of trading hits. Most deaths come from overcommitting to combos with no escape ready, not from a weak character build.

Work through this guide in order — setup, quests, style, then defense — and the early game stops feeling like a maze. Project Slayers rewards players who plan their first twenty levels, so make those levels count, and the rest of the climb takes care of itself.
