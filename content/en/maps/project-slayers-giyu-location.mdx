## Where Does Giyu Spawn in Project Slayers?

Knowing the exact Project Slayers Giyu location can save you a long, dangerous run across the map — and a wasted respawn. Giyu is a late-game boss with a punishing moveset, and he never spawns alone. This guide covers the Project Slayers Giyu location, the fastest route to reach it, the guard problem, and the fight itself.

The short answer: he spawns south of Butterfly Mansion. That landmark is your anchor. If you can find Butterfly Mansion on the main map, you can find Giyu — you just have to survive the walk, because his two guards have an unusually wide aggro range and will pull from much farther away than most players expect.

That detail is why so many players search for the Project Slayers Giyu location and still end up fighting three enemies at once. Treat the approach as part of the boss fight, not as travel time.

Giyu stands in the open with a guard on either side of him. That pattern is the visual confirmation that you have arrived at the right spot — no guesswork required.

## Giyu's Guards and the Safe Hill Strategy

The two guards are the real reason the Project Slayers Giyu location is considered a difficult stop rather than a quick farm. They bodyguard him constantly and react from a long distance, so a careless sprint toward Giyu usually means three enemies hitting you at once.

Community reports offer two reliable approaches, and both involve planning before the pull:

- **Clear the guards first.** Kill both guards, then turn on Giyu. Be careful not to re-engage them after they respawn while you are still fighting him.
- **Split the fight with a partner.** If you have a second player, have them lure both guards to the far side of the hill. With the guards stuck on the wrong side, you can climb the hill and fight Giyu alone.

The hill beside the spawn is the single most valuable piece of terrain here. Standing on it keeps you out of reach of the L/R sword combos, and it gives you room to heal mid-fight. Ranged moves work especially well from that position, since Giyu has no reliable way to punish a player who stays above him.

There is one caveat worth remembering: if you leave the safe interior section of the hill during the fight, the guards can path around and reach you again. Stay put, use ranged attacks, and only drop down when you are ready to commit.

## Beating Giyu: Move-by-Move Counters

Giyu's danger comes from a small number of very high-damage attacks, not from a huge variety of tricks. Almost every failure against him traces back to blocking at the wrong time or standing inside a multi-hit move.

| Move | What It Does | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LRLRL combo | Swings left and right, then lifts you and slams you down | Block only after the third hit; early blocks get broken |
| LLRLR combo | Swings left and right, then slams the ground for a shockwave | Same rule — block on the third hit, then reposition |
| RRLRL combo | Left and right swings finished with a kick | Same timing; watch for the kick at the end |
| Water Surface Slash | Lunges forward and slashes in a circle | Block it, then start your own M1 combo immediately |
| Water Wheel | Spins forward rapidly for multiple hits | Perfect block as he closes in; a single hit can start a chain |
| Constant Flux | Three slashes plus a high-damage lunge finisher | Run. This is a true block-break and can deal around 130 damage |

The pattern to internalize is that his basic M1 strings hit for roughly 20 per hit, and his heavy attacks land in the 40–45 range. Constant Flux is the outlier and the one move you should never try to tank.

Two practical habits separate clean kills from resets:

- **Block late, not early.** His M1 damage breaks guards quickly, so hold block through the third hit rather than from the first.
- **Save one dash for Constant Flux.** The moment you see the windup, run out of range instead of attempting a trade.

If you have a Water Breathing build yourself, note that several of Giyu's attacks are direct references to that style's forms. Learning to read them from the receiving end makes you noticeably harder to hit as a Water user in normal PvP.

Giyu's advantage is timing. A player who only has a short session can farm him during the day cycle without waiting for night, and he can be chained repeatedly because his spawn point is fixed. His disadvantage is the guards: every kill takes longer than a night-time boss fight, and a failed pull can cost you the entire run.

The honest verdict: Giyu is worth farming if you want mastery EXP, want a shot at his haori or katana, or need tier 2 chests during daylight hours. If you are purely chasing chest volume and you have time to spare, the night-time pair can be more efficient per minute once you know their spawns.

Project Slayers itself is a Roblox experience, so your session length and server population will shape which farm makes sense for you. Boss spawns can also feel more contested right after an update, and Project Slayers 2 content has pulled some players into a separate progression track entirely.

## FAQ

**Where exactly is the Project Slayers Giyu location?**
He spawns south of Butterfly Mansion on the main map. Run south from the mansion, look for the small hill beside his spawn point, and approach from high ground so you do not pull both guards at once.

**How many guards does Giyu have, and how far do they aggro?**
He has two guards, and they aggro from a much longer distance than most enemies in the area. Community reports recommend either killing them first or having a partner lure them to the opposite side of the hill before you engage.

**What should I do when Giyu uses Constant Flux?**
Run. It is a true block-break that lands three slashes plus a lunge finisher, and it can deal around 130 damage if every hit connects. Do not try to block through it.

**What does Giyu drop in Project Slayers?**
He drops a Tier 2 Chest on defeat, with an increased chance at the Giyu Haori and Giyu's Water Katana. He also grants 550 player EXP and 1,050 mastery EXP per kill, making him a strong choice for grinding mastery during the day cycle.
